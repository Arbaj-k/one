# One — Global SEO and market rollout

## Positioning
Primary market: global English-speaking individuals who want everyday tasks, notes, projects, documents and personal money records in one workspace.

Canonical remains https://arbaj-k.github.io/one/. Do not change domain or create country folders until there is a real localized product experience and evidence of demand.

## Market sequencing (hypotheses, validate with Search Console)
1. United States, United Kingdom, Canada and Australia — English-first discovery and product feedback.
2. India — English-speaking users; add a separately translated Hindi experience only when the UI, support and product content can support it.
3. Other markets — expand based on impressions, signups and activation rather than assumptions.

These are test markets, not claims of product availability, legal compliance, local support, or pricing in those countries.

## Search intent clusters
- Personal AI assistant for everyday life
- Personal productivity workspace / app
- Daily planner and task manager
- Notes and personal knowledge management
- Personal project organizer
- Personal document organizer
- Expense tracker / personal money records

Avoid competing head-on for broad generic AI or ChatGPT alternative terms until product differentiation and retention justify it. Use feature pages for specific jobs-to-be-done, guides for informational intent, and comparisons for high-intent evaluation.

## Technical international SEO
- Keep one global English URL for each page and a stable self-referencing canonical.
- Set page language to English; use x-default as the global fallback.
- Do not set Open Graph locale to en_IN for a global audience.
- Do not add fake country targeting using geo meta tags, IP redirects, or city names in titles.
- Do not create duplicate /us/, /uk/, /ca/, /au/ pages without genuinely localized content and useful country-specific differences.
- If a future localized version is published, give it a unique URL, translated visible content, reciprocal hreflang links (including itself), and a canonical to itself.
- Keep prices and currency unclaimed until a real published pricing model exists.
- Do not use fabricated reviews, user counts, customer logos, country offices, testimonials or local contact details.

## Content and conversion
- Publish useful, original how-to guides based on real workflows.
- Keep product limitations visible: evolving MVP; AI, cloud sync and document handling depend on current configuration.
- Add screenshots and a real short demo once current UI is stable.
- Make the landing page explain who it is for, what jobs it helps with, and what is currently supported.
- Ensure a clear CTA and a privacy/data limitations link.

## Market measurement
In Google Search Console, compare countries, queries, pages, impressions, clicks, CTR and average position over 28- and 90-day periods. In product analytics, measure visits → signup/guest start → first task/note → 7-day return. Do not choose a country from impressions alone; prioritize activated users and feedback.

## Next operational steps
1. Verify the deployed canonical pages in URL Inspection.
2. Submit the updated sitemap.
3. Review Search Console country/query data after 28 days.
4. Interview a small number of users in the highest-activation country.
5. Only then decide whether to create a localized landing page or translate the product UI.
